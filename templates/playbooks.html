How to Model Real-World "What-If" Scenarios Without Spreadsheet Sprawl

Most retirement calculators force you to clone your entire plan every time you want to test a life decision — meaning any later update to your accounts, salary, or baseline spending has to be manually re-entered into every copy. HVL Family Office uses a Parent Baseline + Child Plan Inheritance model paired with a shared Timeline Event Shelf so you can compare competing life paths side-by-side in seconds.

The Core Mental Model: Baseline = Steady-State Core, Child Plans = Toggleable Life Events

Before creating scenarios, separate your household cash flows into two buckets: your permanent Baseline Plan and toggleable Timeline Event Pins.

1. Baseline Plan (Steady-State Core)Parent Plan

Your Baseline Plan holds your recurring core living budget (ANNUAL LIVING SPEND) plus your primary mortgage balance (which automatically amortizes and drops to zero once paid off). Keep one-off spikes out of this number so Baseline always reflects your true long-term steady-state burn.

2. Shared Event Shelf (Discretionary & Temporary)22 Event Models

Temporary or discretionary costs — K–12 private school, 4-year college tuition, a home rebuild, a sabbatical, pre-65 healthcare, or family gifting — are created once as Event Pins on the shared Event Shelf. Each pin has its own start year, end year, and tax/account drawdown rules.

3. Child Plans (Zero-Maintenance What-Ifs)Live-Synced

Click + Child Plan to branch off Baseline. A child plan automatically inherits all live account balances, equity grants, and baseline settings from its parent — you only override what changes (e.g., retire at 48 instead of 55, or toggle a Home Rebuild pin on).

Playbooks 1 & 2: Steady-State FIRE Burn & Early Retirement Bridges

How to answer "How much do we actually need to retire once the kids graduate and the mortgage is paid off?" and bridge the gap to Medicare at Age 65.

Playbook 1: Isolating True "Steady-State" FIRE Burn Ex-Kids & Ex-Mortgage

If your raw bank/Monarch spending looks high today because of kids, tuition, and a mortgage, don't bake those expiring costs into your permanent retirement living budget:

  1. In Baseline Plan → ▼ Plan Details, set Annual Living Spend to your core recurring lifestyle (excluding mortgage P&I, tuition, and one-off renovations).
  2. Let the engine model your Mortgage P&I and K–12 / 529 College Waterfalls as separate structural layers that automatically step down to zero when the loan pays off and each child graduates.
  3. Create a child plan (e.g., ↳ Full Family Plan + Rebuilds) where you attach discretionary timeline events (Home Renovation, Travel Surge, Weddings).
  4. Click Compare Plans ▼ at the top of Forecasting to see your lean steady-state FI date right next to your fully loaded lifestyle plan.
Total Annual Outflow = Core Living Baseline + Mortgage P&I (until payoff) + Kid/529 Tuition (until graduation) + Active Event Pins

Playbook 2: Early Retirement (Age 45–55) + ACA Bridge & Roth Ladder Pre-65 Optimization

Retiring before age 65 introduces two classic hurdles: paying for healthcare before Medicare eligibility (Age 65) and accessing retirement funds while filling low tax brackets:

  1. Click + Child Plan and name it ↳ Early FIRE at 48. Set Primary/Spouse Retirement Age to 48 and click Save.
  2. Click + Add Event on the Event Shelf and choose Pre-65 Early Retirement Healthcare & ACA Bridge. It automatically starts at your retirement year and ends at Age 65 when Medicare + IRMAA take over.
  3. Leave the event in Dynamic ACA MAGI Subsidy mode so the engine computes your post-retirement MAGI each year and caps healthcare premiums at the statutory ~8.5% MAGI threshold when taxable income is low.
  4. Add a Roth Conversion Ladder event during your post-retirement gap years (before Social Security and RMDs begin at 67–73) to shift pre-tax 401(k)/IRA balances into tax-free Roth space.

Playbooks 3 & 4: Major Capital Decisions, Sankey Cash-Flow Audits & Historical Stress Tests

How to trace every dollar in a complex spike year and stress-test your portfolio against 98 years of market history.

Playbook 3: "Can We Afford a Home Rebuild or Sabbatical?" + Sankey View Single-Year Sankey

When evaluating a major one-off outlay (like a major kitchen/home rebuild, vacation home upgrade, or 1-year unpaid sabbatical), you want to see both the 30-year compounding impact and the exact cash-flow routing in that specific year:

  1. Create a pin on the Event Shelf using the Home Renovation / Remodel (which tracks property equity retention %) or Sabbatical / Career Pivot wizard.
  2. Toggle the pin ON inside a child plan and check the Compare Plans ▼ table to see the exact delta in Age 65 Net Worth, Age 85 Net Worth, and Lifetime Solvency.
  3. Switch the chart lens to Cash Flow (Income vs. Spend) and click Single-Year Sankey in the top-right header.
  4. Click the quick-jump pill for your event year (or drag the timeline scrubber needle) to inspect the proportional Sankey diagram tracing how W-2 income, RSU vests, and portfolio drawdowns fund taxes, core living, mortgage, and the event outflow.
Open Single-Year Sankey → Open Home Remodel Wizard → Sabbatical Wizard →

Playbook 4: 1928–2025 Cohort Backtesting & Dynamic Spending Guardrails Sequence-of-Returns

Assuming a fixed real withdrawal every year regardless of market crashes is unrealistic — in real life, households trim discretionary travel or renovations during a 30% bear market:

  1. Click the Monte Carlo Stress Test lens tab on any scenario.
  2. In the chart header, toggle between Stochastic (1,000 Trials) and 1928–2025 Backtest (98 Cohorts) to replay your plan against every actual historical starting year since 1928 (S&P 500, 10-Yr Treasury, T-Bills, and CPI inflation).
  3. Scroll below the chart to see which exact historical cohorts were toughest (e.g., 1929 Great Crash, 1966–1981 Stagflation, 1973 OPEC Oil Shock, 2000 Dot-Com + GFC).
  4. To see how flexible spending improves resilience, create a child plan, expand ▼ Plan Details → ▼ Advanced Levers, and set Dynamic Spending Guardrails to Moderate Guyton-Klinger (-15% cut when >15% below peak).

Combining multi-state statutory tax law with equity vesting schedules and our 13-tool AI Modeling Assistant.

Playbook 5: Timing a State Relocation Ahead of an Equity / RSU Liquidity Window 50-State Tax Engine

For households in high-tax states (CA, NY, NJ, OR) holding unvested RSUs, ISOs/NSOs, or appreciated stock, relocating domicile before a liquidity window can materially change retirement timing:

  1. Click + Add Event and select State Tax Domicile Relocation (e.g., CA/NY → WA/NV/TX/FL), choosing your target year and destination state.
  2. Pair it on a child plan with a Stock Option Exercise / RSU Liquidity event or a Primary Residence Downsize / Sale.
  3. The engine dynamically switches your state ordinary income and capital gains brackets starting in the relocation year (including state-specific rules like WA's long-term capital gains excise tax).
